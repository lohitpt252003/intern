Making the UDP stream less flaky

The source gives us one 160-byte frame every 20 ms. My sender listens on port 47010, sends coded packets to the relay on 47001, and the receiver finally sends the original frames to the player on 47020.

What I changed

The obvious solution is to resend a packet after it is lost, but it needs a message to go back and then the missing packet to come forward again. That felt too slow for this setup. I instead put four consecutive frames in one group. The sender sends those four frames normally and, after getting the fourth one, sends two extra parity packets.

Normal data packets do not wait for decoding: the receiver forwards them as soon as it gets them. It ignores duplicated rows. If one or two packets are missing, any four of the six rows can rebuild the original four frames using Gaussian elimination over GF(256).

The tradeoff

There are six 165-byte relay packets for every four 160-byte source frames, which works out to roughly 1.55x overhead. There is no feedback traffic. A larger block would add more recovery delay, so I kept the group size at four.

Where it can fail

This code can recover at most two lost packets from the same group. Three losses in one group, or a burst mostly inside one group, will still cause misses. It also cannot help if the packets needed for recovery arrive after the deadline. The selected test delay and results are in RUNLOG.md.
